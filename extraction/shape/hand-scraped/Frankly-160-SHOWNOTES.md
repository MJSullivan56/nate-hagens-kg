**On the heels of Climate Week, Nate poses a thought experiment: What would a world leader’s speech sound like if the difficult truths underpinning global heating – which are politically unsayable due to donors, borders, electoral campaigns, and other incentives – were laid bare to the public? Posing as this hypothetical world leader, Nate traces modern prosperity back to the extraordinary inheritance of ancient sunlight we discovered in the form of fossil fuels. Their extraction and depletion have made today’s civilization possible, while simultaneously bringing forth dire consequences for Earth. He also outlines the deeper behavioral and cultural ways in which billions of individual choices become a system with its own logic and behavior, producing outcomes that no one person intended … or can control. **

**Nate frames this moment in our more-than-human predicament as a test of collective maturity. The challenge we face is recognizing en masse that the energy system that enabled unprecedented material abundance is the same one now threatening the ecological stability we depend on. It also means realizing that there is no single solution or clean reset – instead, shifting the trajectory of the Superorganism requires responses from each of us, accumulated over time, slowly but surely.**

**How exactly did the interests of the system diverge from the interests of the individuals within it? What weight does “collective maturity” carry as a response to the more-than-human predicament? And is it possible for a civilization built around perpetual growth to respond to limits without first experiencing collapse?**

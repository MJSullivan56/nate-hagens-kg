## **Description**

**For years, the climate movement ran on the promise that, if we all rally hard enough, we could have it all: continue with our current system and save the planet. Yet the events of the past few decades have shown that this isn’t quite the case, and in fact, climate is just one crisis among many produced by our growth-at-all-costs system. This raises the question: What happens when a culture built on the certainty of progress has to confront its own uncertainty? **

**In this episode, Nate is joined by philosopher and author Sarah Wilson for an exploration of the emotions that come from learning about, processing, and then embodying the full extent of the metacrisis. Sarah shares her own emotional arc through this journey, and makes the case that “hope” (at least the version most of us think of) is no longer helpful, since it defers responsibility to some future savior rather than requiring anything of us now. In its place, she argues for truth to ground us, revealing the only path forward is to be “thoroughly human” – grieving, laughing, dancing, showing up for our neighbors. She and Nate also explore why prepping alone doesn’t work, and that we should instead be working toward a radical simplification: an intentional shift toward a slower, more communal way of living.**

**Why does the fear of uncertainty run so much deeper than we admit, and what happens to us once we stop resisting it? What emotions and opportunities do we make room for when we let go of false hopes that someone else will solve our crises? And if collapse is actually a slow unraveling of the system we’ve built our lives around, what would it look like to meet it with the same sense of duty and connection that has always seen humans through hard times?**

## **About Sarah Wilson**

**Sarah Wilson is a multiple-time New York Times and Amazon bestselling author, social philosopher, international keynote speaker, minimalist, and philanthropist. She edited Cosmopolitan magazine Australia at 29, founded the global *I Quit Sugar* movement, hosted the most-watched non-sports broadcast TV series in the nation’s history – Masterchef Australia – and wrote the bestseller *First, We Make the Beast Beautiful*, which Mark Manson described as “the best book on living with anxiety that I’ve ever read.”**

**Her book, *This One Wild and Precious Life*, won the US Gold Nautilus Prize, and she has been ranked in the top 200 most influential authors in the world (two years in a row). Her latest book, *I Eat the Stars*, is now available globally.**

**Sarah lives nomadically, leading dynamic, global conversations about modern philosophy, creativity, existential risk and climate change via her keynote speaking, Wild podcast, and her Substack and social communities (of 550,000 subscribers).**
